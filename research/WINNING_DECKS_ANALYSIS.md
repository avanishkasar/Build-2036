## 2. Top 10 patterns from winning decks and repos

1. **Fixed template, fixed order.** Hack2skill and SIH both hand out a template, and judges score against its headings. Winners fill every heading and do not reorder or rename them. SIH says explicitly not to change the pointers.
2. **The first screen states the result, not the logo.** PitchLense opens with the win and live links. Saakh opens with a one line value statement, then live app, video, architecture and impact case. Templates put the team, track and problem on slide 1, because that is how judges file you.
3. **Real screenshots and a live link beat claims.** Saakh has 12 screenshots and a judge mode. SIH templates ask for "images of a working prototype". Winners show the product rather than describe it.
4. **A short demo video, under 3 minutes.** Hack2skill caps it at 3 minutes. Saakh's runs 1:41, with a 24 second teaser on top. The pitch shape that wins: about 20 to 30 s problem, 90 s demo, 30 s impact and ask.
5. **One process flow and one architecture diagram, drawn as pictures.** Every winner has both: PitchLense as images, YouthMind as a service diagram, SIH winners call the flowchart "underrated". Text lists of components score lower.
6. **Problem first, with one cited number.** Templates ask for data or statistics on the problem slide. Winners lead with a person or a real figure and keep the problem short.
7. **Explicit comparison with existing solutions.** Templates ask "how different is it" and what the "limitations of existing commercially available solutions" are. Winners name the alternatives.
8. **Honesty about what is measured.** Saakh has "Testing status" and "Data honesty" sections and writes "I have not timed manual appraisals, so I don't claim a before and after time saving." Judges trust a deck that marks estimates as estimates.
9. **Business viability for the investor stage.** The SIH finale gave 50% to the end to end product and commercial viability, and a Business Model Canvas earned extra credit. TiE juries are VCs who want market, model and an ask.
10. **Light text, one idea per slide.** Templates say "points, diagrams and pictures, not paragraphs". Winning decks keep each slide to a heading plus 3 to 6 short lines. The long detail goes in the README and the speaker notes.

**Slide count:** idea stage decks run 5 to 7 slides (SIH). Hack2skill prototype templates run about 12 to 14 slides. Our 16 is at the top of that range and should not grow past 17 or 18.
